Topic 1 · Accounting Overview

Accounting is an information system serving capital markets.

The AICPA defines accounting as "the art of recording, classifying, and summarizing in a significant manner and in terms of money, transactions and events which are, in part at least, of a financial character, and interpreting the results thereof." In practice, the US system is layered: standard-setters write the rules, regulators enforce them, and the profession applies them under a public-interest mandate.

Standard-Setting

FASB — Private-sector authority for US GAAP

  • FASB (Financial Accounting Standards Board, 1973) issues ASC — the single source of authoritative non-governmental GAAP.
  • GASB sets GAAP for state & local governments.
  • FASAB sets GAAP for US federal entities.
  • Oversight by FAF (Financial Accounting Foundation), trustees appointed independently of preparers.
Regulator

SEC — Statutory authority, delegated to FASB

  • Securities Act 1933 + Exchange Act 1934 give the SEC legal power over accounting for public issuers.
  • SEC formally recognized FASB as the designated standard-setter in Policy Statement, April 2003 (Sarbanes-Oxley §108).
  • SEC retains rule-making power for disclosure (Reg S-X form & content, Reg S-K narrative).
  • Staff guidance: SAB series (e.g., SAB 99 materiality, SAB 101/104 revenue legacy).
Profession

AICPA

Issues the Code of Professional Conduct: Integrity, Objectivity, Independence, Professional Competence & Due Care, Confidentiality, Professional Behavior. Owns the CPA Exam.

Audit Regulator

PCAOB

Created by Sarbanes-Oxley 2002. Registers, inspects, and disciplines auditors of public companies. Sets PCAOB Auditing Standards (e.g., AS 2201 ICFR).

State

State Boards of Accountancy / NASBA

Licensure — the CPA designation is granted by individual states (typically 150 credit-hours + Uniform CPA Exam + 1-2 years experience). NASBA coordinates.

Refs: SOX §108 · FASB ASC 105 · AICPA Code ET §0.300
Topic 2 · Reporting Objective

The objective of general-purpose financial reporting
decision-usefulness for capital providers.

SFAC No. 8, Chapter 1 (OB2): "The objective of general purpose financial reporting is to provide financial information about the reporting entity that is useful to existing and potential investors, lenders, and other creditors in making decisions about providing resources to the entity."

Who is the primary user group?

User classWhat they need
Equity investorsEstimate amount, timing, uncertainty of future net cash inflows; assess management's stewardship.
LendersAssess ability to repay principal + interest when due; evaluate collateral and covenants.
Other creditorsSuppliers, customers (warranty), employees (pensions) — claims on the entity's resources.
Not the primary audienceRegulators, management itself, the general public — they may use the reports but the standards are not written for them.
Stewardship
OB4 (2018 amendment): Decision-usefulness explicitly includes assessing how efficiently and effectively management has discharged its responsibilities. This addresses the long-running debate over whether stewardship is a separate objective — under the current framework, it is part of, not separate from, decision-usefulness.
Key Idea

"General purpose"

One set of reports for many users. The entity does not produce tailored statements per user — that would be impossibly expensive. The framework chooses a primary user group and serves them.

Boundary

What financial reporting does not do

  • Does not show entity value directly.
  • Does not predict future cash flows — provides inputs to that prediction.
  • Cannot meet every user's needs.
SFAC No. 8 Ch.1 OB2–OB11 (2010, amended 2018, 2021)
Topic 2 · Basic Assumptions

Four foundational assumptions under US GAAP.

SFAC 8 Chapter 3 (formerly SFAC 1, 6) and various ASC sections build the report on these four implicit premises. Violate one, and the entire reporting paradigm shifts (e.g., liquidation basis).

① Economic EntityAccounts of the entity are kept separate from those of its owners and from any other entity.
→
② Going ConcernEntity will continue operating for the foreseeable future — at least one year past the issuance date (ASC 205-40).
→
③ PeriodicityIndefinite life can be carved into discrete periods (quarter, year) for reporting.
④ Monetary UnitReported in a single, stable monetary unit (USD for US issuers). Implicitly assumes inflation is not severe enough to require restatement.
Trigger: Liquidation BasisIf liquidation is imminent and not under entity control (ASC 205-30), switch to liquidation basis: assets at estimated cash proceeds, liabilities at expected settlement, plus accrued costs through liquidation.
Going Concern — Management AssessmentEach annual and interim period, management must evaluate whether substantial doubt exists about the entity's ability to continue as a going concern within one year of issuance.
Practitioner note
"Substantial doubt" = probable that the entity will be unable to meet obligations as they become due. If management's mitigation plans are probable of being effectively implemented, no going-concern uncertainty disclosure is required — but the analysis itself is mandatory.
ASC 205-30 · ASC 205-40 · SFAC 8 Ch.3
Topic 3 · Basis of Accounting

GAAP requires accrual basis. Cash basis is a permitted simplification — for very few.

Accrual Basis (GAAP)
  • Recognize revenues when earned (control of goods/services transferred — ASC 606 five-step model).
  • Recognize expenses when incurred (when the underlying obligation arises or asset is consumed).
  • Cash flows are tracked separately on the Statement of Cash Flows (ASC 230).
  • Required for: all public registrants, virtually all GAAP-reporting private companies, all entities issuing audited statements.
Cash Basis / Modified Cash / OCBOA
  • Revenue when cash received; expense when cash paid.
  • Not GAAP — auditors disclose departure or report under special-purpose framework (AICPA's FRF for SMEs, tax basis, regulatory basis).
  • IRS allows cash method for many small businesses (Rev. Proc. 2002-28; expanded under TCJA §263A for taxpayers under the $30M gross receipts threshold — 2025 indexed).
  • Common: solo professionals, small partnerships, certain nonprofits, sole proprietors.

Five-Step Revenue Recognition (ASC 606) — the gold standard of accrual

1. Identify ContractApproval, rights, payment terms, commercial substance, collectibility probable.
2. Identify Performance ObligationsEach distinct good/service the customer benefits from on its own or with readily available resources.
3. Determine Transaction PriceVariable consideration (estimated + constrained), financing component, noncash, consideration payable.
4. Allocate Price to POsRelative standalone selling price; observable, adjusted-market, expected-cost-plus-margin, or residual.
5. Recognize RevenueWhen (or as) control transfers — point in time vs. over time (ASC 606-10-25-27).
Worked example
Acme Software signs a 3-year SaaS contract on Dec 1, 20X1: $360k upfront for one obligation (continuous access). Under accrual: defer the cash, recognize $10k revenue/month over 36 months ratably (control transfers over time). Under cash: book all $360k in Dec 20X1. The accrual version matches revenue to the period of value delivery — exactly what SFAC 8 Ch.1 says investors need.
ASC 606 · ASC 230 · IRS §448 · AICPA FRF for SMEs
Topic 4 · Qualitative Characteristics

What makes financial information useful? Two fundamentals + four enhancers + one constraint.

Fundamental QC — must both be present

① Relevance

Capable of making a difference to a user's decision. Has:

  • Predictive value — input to user's own forecasts.
  • Confirmatory value — confirms or revises prior expectations.
  • Materiality — entity-specific threshold below which omission/misstatement would not change a user's decision (SAB 99).
Fundamental QC — must both be present

② Faithful Representation

Information depicts the economic phenomenon it purports to represent. Three sub-attributes (perfectly would have, but always sought):

  • Complete — all info necessary for user understanding.
  • Neutral — without bias in selection or presentation (with prudence: caution under uncertainty, not deliberate understatement).
  • Free from error — no errors in process or description (does not mean perfectly accurate in every estimate).

Enhancing Qualitative Characteristics — improve usefulness when fundamentals are met

Enhancer ③

Comparability

Similar things look similar, different things look different — across entities (industry peers) and periods (consistency). Not uniformity.

Enhancer ④

Verifiability

Independent observers, using the same method, would reach consensus (not necessarily identical figures, but a reasonable range). Direct (cash count) or indirect (recalc inventory cost flow).

Enhancer ⑤

Timeliness

Information available in time to influence decisions. SEC requires 10-K within 60/75/90 days of FY-end depending on filer size; 10-Q within 40/45 days.

Enhancer ⑥

Understandability

Classified, characterized, presented clearly. Users are assumed to have reasonable knowledge of business and economics and willingness to study with diligence.

The Cost Constraint
SFAC 8 QC35: Benefits of providing information must exceed the cost of providing it. This is a pervasive constraint — not a QC. It explains why disclosure thresholds exist, why small entities have scaled requirements, and why FASB performs cost-benefit analysis before issuing each ASU.
SFAC 8 Ch.3 QC1–QC39 · SAB 99 (Materiality) · ASC 250 (consistency)
Topic 4 · Applying the QCs

Three working examples — where the QCs collide and how preparers resolve.

Conflict 1

Relevance vs. Faithful Representation

Scenario: A mining company holds rare-earth reserves with no active market.

Fair value = highly relevant but hard to verify (poor faithful representation).

Cost = verifiable but stale (poor relevance).

Resolution: GAAP uses cost with impairment testing (ASC 360, 350) — sacrifices some relevance for verifiability. Footnote disclosure restores some relevance.

Conflict 2

Comparability vs. Faithful Representation

Scenario: Tech firm changes useful life of capitalized software from 3 to 5 years based on usage data.

Strictly comparable would freeze the estimate. Faithful representation says reflect new evidence.

Resolution: ASC 250-10 treats this as a change in estimate — prospective application, no retrospective restatement, but disclose the change and its current-period effect.

Conflict 3

Timeliness vs. Verifiability

Scenario: Q3 close — a large tort claim filed Sep 28, no expert assessment yet.

Rushing an estimate harms verifiability; waiting harms timeliness.

Resolution: ASC 450-20 — if loss is reasonably possible, disclose range or fact that no estimate can be made; if probable and estimable, accrue. The framework explicitly permits "no estimate available" as faithful representation.

Loss Contingency Probability Ladder — ASC 450-20

ProbabilityAccrualDisclosure
Probable + estimableAccrue best estimate; if range, accrue lower bound (no point in range more probable).Nature + amount (or that an estimate is made).
Probable + not estimableNo accrual.Nature + statement that estimate cannot be made.
Reasonably possibleNo accrual.Nature + estimate of possible loss or range, or statement that no estimate possible.
RemoteNo accrual.No disclosure (except guarantees per ASC 460).
ASC 250 · ASC 360 · ASC 450 · SFAC 8 QC17–QC18
Topic 5 · Elements (SFAC 8 Ch.4)

Ten elements — the building blocks of the financial statements.

In December 2021, FASB issued SFAC No. 8, Chapter 4 — Elements of Financial Statements, replacing SFAC 6. Definitions were refined to clarify control-based asset definition and present-obligation liability definition.

Element 1

Assets

Present rights of an entity to an economic benefit. Three characteristics: (a) the entity has a right to a benefit, (b) the right confers an economic benefit, (c) the right exists at the reporting date. (Prior definition emphasized "probable future" — current definition is sharper and present-tense.)

Element 2

Liabilities

A present obligation of an entity to transfer an economic benefit. Two characteristics: (a) entity is required to transfer an economic benefit, (b) the requirement is a present obligation from a past transaction or event. No more "probable future sacrifice" — emphasizes obligation exists now.

Element 3

Equity

Residual: Assets − Liabilities. Components per ASC 210/505: contributed capital (par + APIC), retained earnings, treasury stock, AOCI, NCI (ASC 810).

Element 4 & 5

Investments by / Distributions to Owners

Direct equity transactions. Not income. Affect equity but bypass the income statement entirely — this is why dividends are not an expense.

Element 6

Revenues

Inflows or other enhancements of assets, or settlements of liabilities, from delivering or producing goods, rendering services, or other activities that constitute the entity's ongoing major or central operations.

Element 7

Expenses

Outflows, using-up of assets, or incurrence of liabilities from the entity's ongoing major or central operations. Matched to revenues (associated cause-and-effect, systematic allocation, or immediate recognition).

Element 8

Gains

Increases in equity from peripheral or incidental transactions — sale of a fixed asset, FX remeasurement of a non-functional currency balance, derivative MTM not in OCI.

Element 9

Losses

Decreases in equity from peripheral or incidental transactions or from circumstances affecting the entity (e.g., natural disaster write-down, impairment).

Element 10 · Comprehensive Income
Change in equity during a period from transactions and other events from non-owner sources. CI = Net Income + Other Comprehensive Income (OCI). OCI captures items GAAP wants on the balance sheet but not in NI yet: FX translation (ASC 830), AFS debt securities unrealized G/L (ASC 320, post-CECL), pension G/L (ASC 715), cash-flow-hedge effective portion (ASC 815).
SFAC No. 8 Ch.4 (Dec 2021) · ASC 210, 220, 505, 810
Topic 5 · Recognition & Measurement

When does an element get on the books, and at what number?

Recognition criteria — SFAC 5 (still extant) + standard-specific guidance

①

Definition

The item meets the definition of an element of financial statements.

②

Measurability

The item has a relevant attribute measurable with sufficient reliability.

③

Relevance

The information about it is capable of making a difference in user decisions.

④

Faithful Representation

The information is complete, neutral, and free from error.

Five measurement attributes used by US GAAP today

AttributeDefinitionWhere used (examples)
Historical CostCash or cash-equivalent amount paid to acquire the asset at the time of acquisition.PP&E (ASC 360), inventory cost (ASC 330), intangibles initial (ASC 350), most liabilities at issuance.
Current Cost (Replacement)Cash that would be required if the same asset were acquired currently.Inventory LCM/LCNRV test (lower of cost vs. net realizable value, ASC 330-10-35); insurance claims (replacement cost endorsements).
Net Realizable ValueEstimated selling price in the ordinary course of business, less reasonably predictable costs of completion, disposal, and transportation.Inventory ceiling (ASC 330-10-35-1B); receivables net of allowance.
Present Value of Future Cash FlowsDiscounted future cash flows expected from the asset / required for the liability.Pensions (ASC 715), leases right-of-use asset/liability (ASC 842), asset retirement obligations (ASC 410-20), impairment Step 2 (legacy).
Fair ValuePrice that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date (ASC 820).Trading & AFS securities (ASC 320, 321, 825), derivatives (ASC 815), business combinations (ASC 805), goodwill impairment (ASC 350).
Fair Value Hierarchy — ASC 820
Level 1: Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets/liabilities. Level 2: Observable inputs other than Level 1 quotes — similar instruments, observable yield curves, etc. Level 3: Unobservable inputs — entity's own assumptions about market participants' assumptions. Level 3 disclosures (roll-forward, sensitivity) are heavily scrutinized.

Topic 6 · Sustainability / ESG Disclosure

The US ESG disclosure stack — fragmented, evolving, partly voluntary.

Unlike financial reporting (one stack: FASB/ASC), sustainability disclosure in the US blends SEC mandatory rules, voluntary global frameworks, and state-level requirements. As of 2026, the picture is still settling.

Mandatory — SEC

SEC Climate-Related Disclosures (March 2024)

  • Final rule adopted March 6, 2024 — codified in Reg S-K Items 1500–1508 and Reg S-X Article 14.
  • Required: Scope 1 + Scope 2 GHG emissions for LAFs and AFs (when material); climate risks & their material impacts; transition plans; governance & risk management.
  • Scope 3 dropped from final rule (was in proposal).
  • SEC stayed the rule April 2024 pending litigation (5th, 8th Circuit consolidations).
  • Phased compliance originally: LAF FY2025 → AF FY2026 → SRC/EGC FY2027 (now uncertain).
Mandatory — State

California SB 253 & SB 261

  • SB 253 — Climate Corporate Data Accountability Act: companies doing business in CA with revenue > $1B must disclose Scope 1, 2, AND 3 emissions annually, with limited / reasonable assurance.
  • SB 261 — Climate-Related Financial Risk Act: revenue > $500M must report biennial climate financial risk, aligned with TCFD/ISSB.
  • First reports due 2026 (Scope 1+2) and 2027 (Scope 3) after enforcement timeline adjustments.
  • CARB rule-making in progress — administered by California Air Resources Board.
Voluntary global

ISSB / IFRS S1 & S2

Issued June 2023 by the International Sustainability Standards Board. S1: general sustainability-related financial info. S2: climate-specific. Adopted/being adopted in 20+ jurisdictions; not mandatory in the US, but US multinationals subject in subsidiaries abroad.

Voluntary US-origin

SASB Standards

Industry-specific (77 industries) financial-materiality lens. Now consolidated under ISSB via IFRS Foundation (2022 merger of VRF). Heavy US investor uptake; SASB metrics appear in many 10-K Item 1A and proxy statements.

Voluntary global

GRI Standards

Impact materiality lens — what the entity does to the world, not just what the world does to the entity. Complements (rather than competes with) financial-materiality frameworks. Widely used in standalone sustainability reports.

17 CFR 210, 229, 232, 239, 249 (SEC final rule) · CA SB 253/261 · IFRS S1/S2 · SASB · GRI
Topic 6 · Materiality, Pillars, Assurance

How sustainability information enters the financial reporting orbit.

Single vs. Double Materiality

Single (Financial) Materiality
  • SEC, ISSB, SASB lens.
  • "How could the issue affect enterprise value / cash flows?"
  • Investor-facing.
  • Maps to SFAC 8 relevance + materiality (SAB 99).
Double Materiality
  • EU CSRD / ESRS, GRI lens.
  • Includes impact materiality: company's effects on people/planet, regardless of financial feedback loop.
  • Broader stakeholder audience.
  • Not currently US regulatory standard.

TCFD Four Pillars — embedded in ISSB & CA SB 261

  • Governance — board oversight, management's role, committee structure.
  • Strategy — actual & potential impacts on businesses, strategy, financial planning; scenario analysis (e.g., 2°C, 1.5°C).
  • Risk Management — identification, assessment, integration with overall ERM.
  • Metrics & Targets — GHG emissions (Scope 1/2/3), other climate-related metrics; targets and progress.
Assurance Trajectory
US: limited assurance by AICPA member CPAs under SSAE 18 (engagements under AT-C 105/205, 215). CA SB 253 requires limited assurance from FY2026 reports → reasonable assurance from 2030. ISSA 5000 (IAASB, 2024 final) becoming the global benchmark.

Four core ESG content elements expected across frameworks

A

Governance

Board oversight, accountability lines, climate competency, executive compensation links.

B

Strategy

Material risks & opportunities, time horizons (short/medium/long), scenario analysis, capital allocation impact.

C

Risk & Opportunity Management

Identification process, prioritization criteria, integration into ERM, physical & transition risks.

D

Metrics & Targets

Cross-industry metrics (GHG, energy, water), industry-specific SASB metrics, internal targets & progress.

TCFD Final Report 2017 · ISSB S1/S2 · SSAE 18 / AT-C · ISSA 5000
Topic 7 · AI in Accounting & Audit

AI is now a tool in the workpapers — not an exotic add-on.

As of 2026, AI is used across the financial reporting lifecycle. The professional guidance has caught up to the practice in three places: AICPA Technical Q&A, AICPA SOC for AI guidance, and PCAOB inspection focus.

Preparer side — Accounting

Where AI shows up in the books

  • Contract review (ASC 606) — LLMs extract performance obligations, variable consideration, financing components from contracts at scale.
  • Lease ingestion (ASC 842) — auto-classify finance vs. operating, extract discount-rate-relevant terms.
  • Expected credit losses (ASC 326) — ML models for CECL on receivables / loan portfolios; sensitive to validation.
  • Goodwill / asset impairment (ASC 350, 360) — AI-driven trigger detection from external signals.
  • Forecasting — going-concern (ASC 205-40), management's discussion of liquidity (MD&A).
Auditor side — Assurance

Where AI shows up in the audit

  • Risk assessment — anomaly detection across full population of journal entries (replaces sampling at the prelim stage).
  • Confirmations — extraction & matching of confirmation responses at scale.
  • Substantive procedures — full-population recomputation of revenue, payroll, depreciation.
  • Internal control testing — process-mining tools reconstruct actual control execution from log data.
  • ESG assurance — extraction of emissions data from utility bills, fuel receipts, travel logs.
AICPA

Professional ethics & tools

ET §1.295.040 — non-attest services including AI tool deployment to an attest client raise independence concerns. Practitioner must not perform management functions, design systems of internal control, etc. AICPA SOC for AI examinations (2024 conceptual framework) emerging.

PCAOB

Technology-Assisted Analyses

PCAOB Spotlight on Auditor Use of Technology (multiple editions); 2024 staff observations on TAAs. Auditor must still meet AS 1105 sufficient appropriate audit evidence — AI output requires evaluation, not blind acceptance. Documentation of model, inputs, validation, and human review is mandatory.

SEC / SOX

ICFR over AI-generated numbers

If AI feeds figures into the financial statements (e.g., CECL allowance), management's SOX 404 assessment must cover the AI as part of ICFR: input controls, model governance, output review, change management. Auditor's AS 2201 ICFR audit must follow.

Five requirements that show up in every AI-in-accounting framework
① Data quality — garbage in, garbage out, plus bias control. ② Model governance — version control, change approval, ownership. ③ Validation — independent technical review, back-testing, challenger models. ④ Explainability — output traceable to inputs; users can understand why. ⑤ Human oversight — accountable person reviews, signs off, overrides when warranted.

Synthesis · How the seven topics fit together

From objective to recognition to disclosure.

The Why Decision-usefulness for investors, lenders, other creditors — including stewardship assessment (SFAC 8 Ch.1).
The Lens Two fundamental QCs (Relevance + Faithful Rep) filter information; four enhancers improve it; cost constrains it.
The Stuff Ten elements (SFAC 8 Ch.4) categorize what is reported; recognition + measurement decide when and how much.
The Discipline Accrual basis, going concern, periodicity, economic entity, monetary unit assumptions hold the structure together.
The Expansion Sustainability info extends financial reporting into climate/social/governance — SEC + state + global frameworks.
The Tool AI shifts how preparers compute and auditors verify — but human accountability, ICFR, and professional ethics still apply.

Three sentences worth memorizing

  1. "Decision-usefulness, not stewardship in isolation" — the modern US framework treats them as one objective, with stewardship a component.
  2. "Relevance + Faithful Representation are both required" — neither alone is sufficient; enhancers cannot fix a deficit in either.
  3. "GAAP is what FASB says it is, under SEC delegation, enforced by PCAOB-registered auditors, applied by AICPA-credentialed CPAs." The whole US apparatus in one sentence.
