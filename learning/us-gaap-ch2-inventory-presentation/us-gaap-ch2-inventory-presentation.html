Topic 01 · ASC 330-10-20 · Glossary · Master Glossary

What qualifies as Inventory under US GAAP

ASC 330-10-20 defines inventory as personal property items held for sale in the ordinary course of business, items in process of production for such sale, or items to be currently consumed in the production of goods or services to be available for sale.

In Scope
Inventory categories
  • Finished Goods — completed, held for sale
  • Work-in-Process (WIP) — partially completed
  • Raw Materials — to be consumed in production
  • Manufacturing Supplies — directly consumed
  • Merchandise — purchased for resale (retail/wholesale)
  • Goods in transit when title has passed (FOB shipping point: buyer; FOB destination: seller until delivered)
  • Consigned goods — remain on consignor's books, not the consignee's
ASC 330-10-20 · ASC 845-10-30 (transfer of control)
Out of Scope
Not inventory under ASC 330
  • Long-lived assets held for use → ASC 360 (PP&E)
  • Self-constructed plant assets → ASC 360
  • Service-provider work-in-process per ASC 606 contract assets → ASC 340-40 contract costs
  • Spare parts & maintenance materials for capital equipment, when classified as PP&E components
  • Biological assets / agricultural produce at point of harvest → ASC 905 (agriculture)
  • Investments in securities held for trading → ASC 320 / 321
ASC 330-10-15 · ASC 905-330 · ASC 340-40-25
Recognition principle
Inventory is recognized when an entity obtains control (ASC 606-10-25-30 mirrored for purchase side: ability to direct use and obtain substantially all remaining benefits, including risk of obsolescence and price decline). Consignment arrangements fail this test for the consignee.
Topic 02 · ASC 330-10-30 · IRC §263A (UNICAP)

Initial Cost Measurement

ASC 330-10-30-1: "the primary basis of accounting for inventories is cost, which has been defined generally as the price paid or consideration given to acquire an asset." Cost includes all expenditures necessary to bring inventory to its existing condition and location ready for sale.

Capitalizable Costs
What goes into inventory
  • Invoice price net of trade and cash discounts
  • Freight-in, insurance in transit, duties, non-recoverable taxes
  • Direct labor and applied manufacturing overhead
  • Allocable fixed production overhead based on normal capacity (ASC 330-10-30-3, FASB Statement 151)
  • Material handling, receiving, inspection costs before "ready for sale"
ASC 330-10-30-1 to 30-8
Period-Expensed
Excluded from inventory cost
  • Abnormal idle facility expense — excess of actual fixed overhead over normal capacity → COGS (ASC 330-10-30-7)
  • Abnormal spoilage, freight, handling, waste → expensed currently
  • Selling expenses, general administration
  • Storage costs after production complete (unless required for production process)
  • Advertising and re-handling of finished goods
ASC 330-10-30-7 · SFAS 151 (2004)
IRC § 263A · Uniform Capitalization (UNICAP)
For tax, the IRS requires capitalization of a broader set of indirect costs than GAAP — including general administrative costs allocable to production. This creates routine book-tax temporary differences (ASC 740 deferred tax). The 2017 TCJA exempts businesses with avg gross receipts ≤ $25M (indexed; $30M for 2024).

Normal capacity rule (ASC 330-10-30-3)

Allocation of fixed production overhead is based on normal capacity of the production facility — the production expected to be achieved over a number of periods under normal circumstances. The amount allocated to each unit is not increased due to abnormally low production or idle plant. Unallocated overhead is recognized as expense in the period incurred.

Topic 03 · ASC 330-10-30-9 to 30-14 · IRC § 472

Cost-Flow Assumptions

ASC 330-10-30-9: "cost for inventory purposes may be determined under any one of several assumptions as to the flow of cost factors." Selection is an accounting policy disclosure (ASC 330-10-50-1).

MethodMechanicsWhen usedRising prices impact
Specific Identification Each unit's actual cost tracked individually Unique, high-value, low-volume goods (cars, art, jewelry); customized inventory Most accurate matching
FIFO (First-In, First-Out) Oldest costs flow to COGS first; newest costs in ending inventory Default for most non-LIFO US filers; required under IFRS Lower COGS · higher net income · higher ending inventory
Weighted-Average Periodic: avg cost = (BI + purchases) ÷ total units
Perpetual ("moving average"): recalc after each purchase		 Homogeneous, commingled inventory; commodities Smooths COGS · between FIFO and LIFO
LIFO (Last-In, First-Out) Newest costs to COGS first; oldest costs remain in ending inventory ("LIFO layers") US-only (allowed by ASC 330 + IRC § 472); IFRS prohibits Higher COGS · lower net income · lower taxes · "LIFO Reserve" disclosed
IRC § 472 · LIFO Conformity Rule
If a taxpayer uses LIFO for tax, it must also use LIFO for primary financial reporting (book conformity). However, the entity must disclose the LIFO reserve so users can recompute as if FIFO were used. LIFO Reserve = FIFO inventory − LIFO inventory.
LIFO Reserve disclosure

SEC Reg S-X Item 5-02 and ASC 330-10-50-1(b) require disclosure of the excess of replacement (or current) cost over LIFO carrying value. Analysts add this back to compare LIFO filers with FIFO peers (e.g., ratio of inventory turnover, gross margin).

LIFO Liquidation

When ending inventory units fall below beginning units, old (low-cost) LIFO layers are matched to current-period revenue, producing an artificial profit spike. SEC SAB Topic 5.NN requires disclosure of "LIFO liquidation effect" when material.

Topic 04 · ASC 330-10-35 · ASU 2015-11 (July 2015)

Subsequent Measurement — LCNRV vs LCM

In July 2015 FASB issued ASU 2015-11, simplifying inventory measurement: entities not on LIFO or retail inventory method now use Lower of Cost or Net Realizable Value (LCNRV). LIFO and retail method filers remain on the legacy Lower of Cost or Market (LCM).

LCNRV (Post-ASU 2015-11)
For FIFO, average cost, specific ID

NRV = Estimated selling price in the ordinary course of business − reasonably predictable costs of completion, disposal, and transportation.

Compare unit-by-unit (or category if individual unit infeasible) cost to NRV. Recognize loss when cost > NRV; write down to NRV.

Dr. Cost of Goods Sold (or Inventory Loss)
Cr. Inventory
ASC 330-10-35-1B to 35-7
LCM (Pre-ASU 2015-11 · still LIFO/Retail)
"Middle of three" rule

Market = Replacement cost, bounded by:
Ceiling = NRV (selling price − completion/disposal costs)
Floor = NRV − normal profit margin

Inventory carried at lower of cost vs market (the bounded replacement cost).

ASC 330-10-35 (legacy paragraphs for LIFO/retail filers)

Write-down rules common to both

  • Write-down to NRV (or market) creates a new cost basis — ASC 330-10-35-14
  • Reversal of a prior write-down is PROHIBITED under US GAAP, unlike IFRS (IAS 2 §33), even if NRV later recovers — ASC 330-10-35-14
  • Write-downs are typically recorded in COGS unless of such magnitude that they must be separately disclosed
  • Material write-downs require disclosure of the amount, the location in the income statement, and the reason (ASC 330-10-50-2)
Key US vs IFRS divergence
IFRS (IAS 2) requires LCNRV for all inventory and requires reversal of prior write-downs (capped at original cost) when conditions reverse. US GAAP prohibits reversal — write-down is permanent and establishes a new cost basis.
Topic 05 · ASC 330-10-30-12 to 30-15 · ASC 270 (interim)

Estimation Methods — Retail · Gross Profit · Standard Cost

When perpetual inventory tracking is impractical, GAAP allows three estimation techniques — each with distinct applications and limitations.

Retail Inventory Method
For high-volume retail

Maintains records at both cost and retail values. Cost-to-retail ratio is applied to ending inventory at retail.

Cost % = Cost ÷ Retail
Ending Inv (cost) = Ending Inv (retail) × Cost %

Variants: Conventional (LCM, includes markups, excludes markdowns in ratio); LIFO Retail; Dollar-Value LIFO Retail.

ASC 330-10-30-12 · IRS §1.471-8
Gross Profit Method
For interim reporting · casualty loss

Uses historical gross margin % to estimate ending inventory from sales:

COGS = Sales × (1 − GM%)
End Inv = BI + Purchases − COGS

Not acceptable for annual financial statements — only interim, internal, or estimating inventory destroyed in fires/theft (insurance claims).

ASC 270 (interim) · not GAAP for annual
Standard Cost
For manufacturers

Pre-determined standards for material, labor, overhead. Variances analyzed.

Acceptable under GAAP only if standards are reviewed regularly and adjusted to approximate actual cost (ASC 330-10-30-12). Significant variances must be allocated to inventory and COGS.

ASC 330-10-30-12

Dollar-Value LIFO (DV LIFO)

For multi-SKU inventories under LIFO. Inventory is grouped into pools; LIFO layers are measured in dollars (not units) using price indexes (internal or external — CPI, PPI, or company-specific). Reduces LIFO bookkeeping burden and protects from involuntary LIFO liquidation when individual SKUs change.

ASC 330-10-30-9 to 30-14 · IRS Reg §1.472-8
Topic 06 · ASC 606-10-25 · ASC 845 · ASC 330-10-25

Transfer of Title, Freight, Consignment

Whether goods belong on the buyer's or seller's balance sheet at period end turns on control transfer under ASC 606-10-25-30 — the same indicators used for revenue recognition.

FOB Shipping Point
Buyer takes title at seller's dock
  • Title transfers when goods leave seller's premises
  • Buyer includes goods in inventory while in transit
  • Buyer pays freight (Freight-In → capitalized into inventory cost)
  • Buyer bears risk of loss in transit
FOB Destination
Seller retains title until delivery
  • Title transfers only when goods arrive at buyer
  • Seller keeps goods in inventory while in transit
  • Seller pays freight (Freight-Out → period expense, selling cost)
  • Seller bears risk of loss in transit

Consignment arrangements (ASC 606-10-55-79 to 55-80)

Goods on consignment are NOT inventory of the consignee — they remain on the consignor's balance sheet. Indicators of consignment include:

  • The dealer is not obligated to pay until the product is sold to a third party
  • The entity is able to require return or transfer of the product to another dealer
  • The dealer does not have unconditional obligation to pay (no unconditional sale)

Consignor recognizes revenue only when the consignee sells to the end customer. Cost of consigned goods plus freight to consignee remains capitalized as inventory until sale.

Right of return (ASC 606-10-32-10)

When customers have a right of return, the seller recognizes revenue net of expected returns and records a refund liability and a corresponding right to recover assets returned (an asset) — measured at the former carrying amount of inventory less expected recovery costs.

Topic 06 · Physical Inventory · ASC 330-10-35 · AS 2510

Physical Counts, Shrinkage, Obsolescence

ASC 330 does not prescribe a count method, but PCAOB AS 2510 — Auditing Inventories requires auditors to observe physical counts. Discrepancies between perpetual records and physical count drive an adjusting entry.

Normal shrinkage

Expected loss from evaporation, breakage, theft within typical limits. Charged to COGS as part of normal operations.

Dr. Cost of Goods Sold
Cr. Inventory
Abnormal shrinkage / losses

Material losses from casualty, fraud, or other unusual events. Charged to a separate line — Loss on Inventory — and disclosed when material.

Dr. Loss on Inventory (operating, separately stated)
Cr. Inventory

Obsolescence reserve (excess and obsolete — "E&O")

Entities typically maintain a contra-inventory reserve calculated by aging buckets (e.g., goods with no demand in last 12/24 months) or specific identification of obsolete SKUs. The reserve estimate is a critical accounting estimate requiring disclosure under SEC Reg S-K Item 303(b)(3) (MD&A) when material.

Dr. Cost of Goods Sold (or Inventory Write-down Expense)
Cr. Allowance for Obsolescence (contra-inventory)
SAB 100 · "Cookie Jar" reserves
SEC Staff Accounting Bulletin No. 100 warns against using obsolescence reserves to smooth earnings. Reserves must be supported by specific facts and circumstances, not vague "general business risk." Once written down, the reduced carrying amount is a new cost basis — no reversal even if items later become saleable.

Inventory counts under SOX § 404

Internal controls over inventory existence (cycle counts, year-end physical counts), valuation (LCNRV review, obsolescence reserve methodology), and cutoff (FOB tracking) are key audited control areas. Material weaknesses here often trigger restatements.

Over-time recognition (ASC 606-10-25-27)

For inventory produced under customer-specific contracts where the customer controls WIP as it is produced (e.g., highly customized goods with no alternative use + enforceable right to payment), revenue is recognized over time using an input or output method. WIP is reclassified from inventory to contract asset (ASC 606-10-45-3).

Bill-and-hold (ASC 606-10-55-81 to 55-84)

Revenue can be recognized before physical delivery only if: (a) reason for arrangement is substantive (customer requested); (b) product is identified separately; (c) product is ready for physical transfer; (d) entity cannot use product or direct it to another customer.

Principal vs Agent (ASC 606-10-55-36)

An entity is a principal if it controls the good before transfer to the customer — recognizes gross revenue and holds inventory. An agent recognizes net commission and does not record inventory.

Comparison · ASC 330 vs IAS 2

US GAAP vs IFRS — five critical divergences

For multi-jurisdictional reporting, the differences below drive most reconciling items between US 10-K and IFRS annual report inventory balances.

TopicUS GAAP (ASC 330)IFRS (IAS 2)
LIFO permitted? Yes — allowed under ASC 330; required for book if used for tax (IRC §472) No — prohibited (IAS 2 §25)
Subsequent measurement LCNRV (non-LIFO) post-ASU 2015-11; LCM still allowed for LIFO and retail method LCNRV for all inventory (IAS 2 §9)
Reversal of write-downs Prohibited — new cost basis (ASC 330-10-35-14) Required when conditions reverse, capped at original cost (IAS 2 §33)
Interest capitalization Generally not for inventory; only for assets produced as discrete projects over substantial period (ASC 835-20-15-5) For "qualifying assets" only — generally excludes inventory produced in large quantities on repetitive basis (IAS 23 §4)
Same items, single measurement basis? Different methods (FIFO, LIFO, average) for different inventory items allowed if justified by use Same cost formula must be applied to all inventories with similar nature and use (IAS 2 §25)

Practical reconciliation example

A US LIFO filer reporting under IFRS would: (1) eliminate LIFO entirely → use FIFO or weighted-average; (2) reverse the LIFO reserve through opening retained earnings; (3) re-evaluate prior write-downs for potential reversal; (4) potentially exclude certain interest costs that had been capitalized.

Note
Roughly 15-20% of S&P 500 filers reported some inventory on LIFO as of 2024 (down from over 40% in the 1980s). The decline reflects (a) growing tax pressure to switch, (b) IFRS convergence considerations, and (c) lower inflation reducing LIFO's tax benefit.
Bonus · Technology · AI · Supply Chain

Technology in inventory accounting & assurance

Modern ERPs (SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics) maintain perpetual records with real-time cost-flow logic. AI and ML are increasingly used in both preparer and auditor workflows, with new governance considerations.

Preparer side
AI/ML in inventory management
  • Demand forecasting — ML models predict reorder points, reducing E&O reserves
  • Dynamic NRV estimation — pulling marketplace prices, FX, freight indices in real time
  • Obsolescence detection — anomaly detection on slow-moving SKUs
  • Computer-vision physical counts — drones, robots, RFID combined with image recognition
  • Predictive shrinkage — pattern detection across stores/warehouses
Auditor side
Audit data analytics (ADA)
  • 100% population analytics on inventory aging, margin by SKU, days-on-hand
  • Computer-assisted audit techniques (CAATs) for cost re-performance
  • Drone-assisted physical observation under PCAOB AS 2510
  • Continuous controls monitoring of cutoff transactions around period-end

Governance requirements (AICPA + PCAOB + NIST AI RMF 1.0)

AICPA Code ET §1.295 — non-attest services
Auditors may not design or implement the client's inventory AI/ML models that drive material accounting estimates — would impair independence. Audit firms separate ADA (audit use) from advisory (client implementation) staff and engagements.
Synthesis · 6 frames + 3 takeaways

The inventory chapter in one screen

Why
Why inventory matters

For most non-financial companies, inventory is the largest current asset and the dominant driver of working capital, gross margin, and tax expense. Inventory misstatements are a top-3 cause of public-company restatements per Audit Analytics annual reports.

Lens
The 3 measurement gates

1. Initial — capture all costs to "ready for sale" (ASC 330-10-30)
2. Subsequent — write down to NRV when impaired (35)
3. Derecognition — match to revenue at control transfer (ASC 606)

Stuff
The 4 cost-flow choices

Specific ID (unique), FIFO (default), Weighted-Average (commodity), LIFO (US-only tax tool). Choice is policy disclosure; consistency required.

Discipline
The asymmetry

Write-downs hit P&L immediately. Reversals prohibited (US GAAP) — new cost basis. This conservatism matters at impairment points (recessions, obsolete tech).

Expansion
Adjacent standards

ASC 606 (revenue/COGS recognition), ASC 360 (PP&E for self-constructed assets), ASC 740 (book-tax UNICAP differences), ASC 470 (pledged collateral), ASC 845 (consignment).

Tool
SOX & audit hooks

Cycle counts, cutoff procedures, NRV reserves, obsolescence reserves. PCAOB AS 2510 audit; SAB 100 reserve discipline; SEC critical accounting estimate MD&A.

Three sentences to memorize

1. Inventory is held for sale, valued at the lower of cost or NRV, and recognized as COGS when control passes — everything else is mechanics.

2. Write-downs are permanent under US GAAP; choose LCNRV unless on LIFO/retail; document the methodology because it's a critical accounting estimate.

3. LIFO is a US tax artifact — IFRS bans it. If you see "LIFO Reserve" and "LIFO Liquidation" in the 10-K, the company is filing a US-only return.
